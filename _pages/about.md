Hi, I'm Rachel Zoll.

I'm an engineer with a background in microrobotics, medical devices, and semiconductor hardware. I recently completed my **Ph.D. in Engineering Sciences at [Harvard's Microrobotics Laboratory](https://www.micro.seas.harvard.edu)**, where I was advised by [Prof. Robert J. Wood](https://seas.harvard.edu/person/robert-wood) and funded by an NSF Graduate Research Fellowship. My thesis focused on millimeter-scale multi-material manufacturing for biomedical devices, including a spring-driven capsule robot for GI fluid sampling and a soft-elastomer mechanism for fastener-free tissue anchoring.

Before Harvard, I earned my **B.S. and M.S. in EECS at UC Berkeley**, where I was a researcher in the Pister and Maharbiz labs working on ASIC tapeouts and MEMS-actuated neural recording electrodes, and interned at **Facebook**, **Intel**, **FMC Schilling Robotics**, and the **US Naval Research Laboratory**. I've also worked in industry and clinical settings — leading hardware development for an autonomous feeding tube device through two in vivo swine trials at MGH & BWH, and supporting technical due diligence across a $50M+ portfolio of deep-tech innovations at Harvard's Office of Technology Development & ARPA-Health.
